# Top 10 Medical-AI Marketing Sites: Research Memo for the Drawbridge Website

- **Prepared for:** Drawbridge marketing site (MOATiT, LLC)
- **Access date for all sources:** 2026-10-16
- **Method:** I fetched each homepage, plus pricing, security, trust and demo pages where they exist. I also searched the web for traction evidence such as funding and customer counts. I paraphrased all page copy. Direct quotes are limited to a few words.
- **Caveat on "best-converting":** Conversion rates are not public. I used traction as a proxy for marketing effectiveness: recent funding, valuation, customer or clinician counts, and category leadership. Pages were read as extracted text, so client-side elements may be missing from the notes, including HubSpot or Chili Piper forms, logo images and animated counters. Treat form field counts as unverified unless a count is stated.

## 3. Per-site notes

### 3.1 Tennr: tennr.com
- **Traction:** $101M Series C led by IVP in June 2025, about $605M post-money. Investors include ICONIQ, GV, a16z, Lightspeed and YC (AuntMinnie; Axios Pro; Fundz).
- **Positioning:** An agentic platform that reads incoming referral documents, checks them against payer criteria and keeps patients moving to care.
- **Hero pattern:** All-caps eyebrow promise (right patient, right setting), a 3-word verb headline, and a one-sentence platform subhead. One hero CTA, plus "Book a Demo" in the header.
- **Proof:** Customer logo strip ("trusted nationwide"). Three named testimonials with full titles (NEB Medical, Total Sleep Management, King Drug). Three case studies with outcome headlines (75% faster referrals, 4x volume, 3-week backlog to same-day intake). A customer anecdote about clearing 260 faxes in a day. A press logo bar (Fortune, Forbes, Axios, Bloomberg, TechCrunch).
- **CTAs:** "Book a Demo" in the header, "Learn More" in the hero (goes to demo), "Explore the Product" mid-page, and "Get Started" twice. Closes with "Let's work together." Every CTA ends at the same demo page.
- **Pricing:** None.
- **Security:** HIPAA and SOC 2 badges in the footer only. No body copy and no linked trust page.
- **Demo flow:** Book-a-demo page. The form is client-rendered, so fields were not captured. No video or interactive demo.
- **Structure:** Webinar banner, hero, "at a glance" explainer, 3 value pillars, logos, outreach and phone, automation list, testimonials, 5 capability blocks (intake, missing docs, insurance, payer, "control tower"), case studies, press, closing CTA, footer.
- **Notable extra:** A humorous "Fax It" storytelling landing page. It argues that fax isn't the problem; the intake backlog is. Strong category framing for a fax-heavy audience.
- **Borrow:** Before-and-after case-study headlines in backlog terms (weeks of backlog to same day) and the "fax isn't the problem, the backlog is" reframe.
- **Avoid:** Vague "Learn More" as the hero CTA, and security reduced to footer badges with no explanation.

### 3.2 Notable: notablehealth.com
- **Traction:** About $100M round at a $600M valuation (circa 2021) from F-Prime, Oak HC/FT, ICONIQ and Greylock (SmartBranding/Craft). Its SafeBase trust center lists HITRUST, SOC 2 Type 2, ISO 27001:2022 and PCI DSS.
- **Positioning:** An AI agent platform built for healthcare that removes manual work across patient access, revenue cycle and care operations.
- **Hero pattern:** Category-claim headline, scope subhead, and two CTAs (Request a demo, Watch video).
- **Proof:** Enterprise logo strip (CommonSpirit, MUSC, UCSD, cityMD, Optum and others). A dense outcome grid that includes referral turnaround cut from 14 to 3 days, 97% of in-scope referrals automated and 85% of order transcription automated. Success stories for Florida Health System (referrals) and CommonSpirit. Weakness: metrics are not clearly attributed to specific customers.
- **CTAs:** "Request a demo" in the header and hero. The closing section uses a newsletter email capture rather than a demo CTA.
- **Pricing:** None.
- **Security:** A "Trust & Security" link sits in the **header**, plus a Security Center link and SOC and ISO badges in the footer. The SafeBase trust center has sections for compliance, risk profile, product security, downloadable reports gated by request-access, a CAIQ self-assessment, legal documents, infrastructure (GCP) and an SSL grade.
- **Demo flow:** Request-a-demo page (client-rendered form). Webinar replays serve as an interactive substitute.
- **Structure:** Hero, logos, 3 use-case cards, platform (low-code builder), results grid, resources, closing subscribe, footer.
- **Borrow:** The trust center pattern: a public summary of controls, with reports available on request, linked from the main nav.
- **Avoid:** Unattributed metric grids, and ending the page on a newsletter form instead of the conversion action.

## 4. Synthesis: top 12 things to borrow

1. **Name the buyer in the hero.** Freed names independent clinics, and Hello Patient promises growth without adding staff. The strongest SMB heroes say who the product is for and which pain it removes in one line.
2. **Put the interactive "hear it, see it" demo directly under the hero.** Examples: Hello Patient's simulated call with live transcript, Assort's call-this-number line and Infinitus's call transcripts. For a voice product, this is the single most persuasive element.
3. **Use a dual CTA: one low-friction self-serve action plus one sales action.** Examples: Try It + Book a Call (Hello Patient) and Try for free + Contact sales (Freed). Keep the same labels in the sticky header and the closing section.
4. **Pair every proof point with a specific number and a source.** Abridge pairs a logo with a metric, Assort pairs a named executive with a $ or % result, and Tennr uses backlog-to-same-day headlines.
5. **State outcomes in backlog and capacity terms, not "AI" terms.** Tennr's "3-week backlog to same-day" and Hello Patient's "3 more appointments per location per day" are concrete and operational.
6. **Show security in the body of the page, not only the footer.** Freed puts badges in the hero, Hello Patient has a dedicated privacy section and Infinitus explains its guardrails on the homepage.
7. **Publish a real trust center.** Notable (SafeBase) and Infinitus show public controls, documents available on request and a linked Security page with concrete specs such as TLS versions, encryption at rest, BAAs, pen tests and vulnerability reporting.
8. **Explain how the AI is kept safe.** Infinitus describes scripted verbatim language, pre-approved knowledge, clinician review and AI self-identification on calls. AKASA lets the customer set the autonomy threshold.
9. **Be transparent about price for the SMB segment.** Freed is the only site with published prices and the most self-serve-oriented, and it targets the same small-practice buyer as Drawbridge.
10. **Show integration logos, with the buyer's EHR in them.** Hello Patient shows Veradigm. Abridge's form asks for the EMR. Practices first want to know whether it works with their system.
11. **List the concrete jobs the agent does.** Assort lists about 15 tasks such as scheduling, refills and lab requests. Tennr lists intake, missing documents and eligibility. A plain task list beats abstract platform language.
12. **Add "what to expect" copy at the demo step, plus risk reversal.** Assort describes a 30-minute walkthrough with real recordings and says there is no pressure. Freed offers a 7-day trial with no card and human onboarding.

## 5. Top 8 things to avoid

1. **Vague CTAs** such as "Learn More" (Ambience, Tennr hero) and label drift ("Let's Chat" vs "Start Now" vs "Find out more" at AKASA).
2. **Inconsistent numbers across sections.** Assort shows 81% vs 75% for the same customer, and Freed's retention and SOC wording varies. Every claim must match everywhere.
3. **Unattributed or unsourced metrics.** Notable's grid and Infinitus's "up to 5x ROI" give no methodology.
4. **Animated counters that render as "0"** without JavaScript (AKASA, Ambience). They hurt SEO and look broken to crawlers and screen readers.
5. **Badges with no explanation and no trust page** (Tennr, Hello Patient), or no security story at all (Assort, Abridge, AKASA) for a product that handles PHI.
6. **Anonymous testimonials** ("VP at a top-10 pharma"). They add little credibility, and Drawbridge should not fabricate even anonymized ones.
7. **Empty sections and placeholder links** (Develop Health's blank audience cards, Notable's and Assort's "#" video and try-it links).
8. **Long qualification forms for small buyers** (Abridge's 7-field multi-step form) and closing the page on a newsletter field instead of the main CTA (Notable).

## 6. Recommendations for Drawbridge

Constraints: no customer logos or testimonials yet. Every claim must be verifiable. Synthetic-test numbers must be labeled as such. Veradigm references must respect whatever approval and certification status Musab's process has reached. Do not imply partnership or certification before it exists, and use only the trademark wording Veradigm approves.

### 6.1 Hero pattern
**Structure:** outcome headline, a subhead naming the user and the EHR, two CTAs, then a trust strip (facts, not logos).

- **Headline (draft):** "Every fax filed. Every call answered." (two jobs, two outcomes)
- **Subhead (draft):** AI for small and mid-sized practices on Veradigm. Drawbridge reads incoming faxes and lab results and files them to the right patient chart. Its 24/7 phone assistant verifies callers and books or reschedules visits.
- **CTAs:** primary **"Book a 20-minute walkthrough"**, secondary **"Hear the phone assistant"** (scrolls to the demo).
- **Trust strip below the CTAs:** "HIPAA BAA available", "Built for Veradigm EHR/PM" (wording subject to Veradigm approval), "Shadow-mode pilot before go-live" and "Human review below the confidence threshold". Only include items that are true on launch day.
- **Hero visual:** a split visual of a fax thumbnail becoming a filed chart entry, beside a call transcript snippet. Use synthetic data, labeled as sample.

### 6.2 Section order
1. Sticky header: Product (Fax & Lab Intake, Phone Assistant), How it works, Security, Pricing, plus the **Book a walkthrough** button.
2. Hero (as above).
3. **Live demo band:** "Hear it" (a recorded or simulated call using synthetic patient data, with a transcript) and "See it" (an animated fax-to-chart walkthrough). Optionally publish a demo phone line that only books fake appointments in a sandbox.
4. **The problem, in practice terms:** the fax pile, after-hours voicemail and hold times. Cite neutral industry sources only (MGMA, CAQH Index, published studies), each with a link.
5. **Two product sections in workflow order.**
   - Fax and lab intake: receive, read and classify, match the patient by name and DOB plus secondary identifiers, file to Veradigm, route exceptions to a human queue.
   - Phone assistant: answer, verify identity, book or reschedule, write back to the PM, hand off to staff.
6. **What Drawbridge handles:** a concrete task checklist, plus an "escalates to your staff when..." list.
7. **How the AI stays safe:** confidence thresholds the practice controls (borrowed from AKASA), the review queue, an audit log of every filing and call, the assistant identifying itself as AI and a clear path to a human.
8. **Results so far (honest proof):** synthetic-test metrics in clearly labeled cards. Each card says "Synthetic test, N = __ de-identified or simulated documents, run on [date]" and links to a methodology page. Pilot results can replace these later.
9. **Rollout:** a 1-week shadow mode (Drawbridge suggests, staff confirm), then go-live, with a named human onboarding contact. This serves as risk reversal in place of testimonials.
10. **ROI calculator** (see 6.5).
11. **Security and HIPAA summary** with a link to the trust center.
12. Pricing or "how pricing works" (see 6.6).
13. FAQ covering patient-consent wording for AI calls, what happens when the AI is unsure, data retention, whether PHI is used to train models (state the real answer), and EHR versions supported.
14. **Founder note** (borrowed from Freed). Who MOATiT is and why it built this. A credible substitute for logos at launch.
15. Closing CTA that repeats the hero CTAs, then the footer (Security, HIPAA, Privacy, Terms, Accessibility, Contact).

### 6.3 CTA labels (use consistently)
- Primary: **"Book a 20-minute walkthrough"** (header, hero, mid-page and close; the same label everywhere).
- Secondary: **"Hear the phone assistant"** and **"See a fax get filed"** (demo anchors).
- Tertiary: **"Calculate your time savings"** (ROI) and **"Read our security overview"**.
- Avoid: "Learn More", "Let's Chat", "Get Started" (ambiguous) and any "Free trial" until a self-serve path really exists.

### 6.4 Trust center design
Use a page at `/security` (and later a hosted portal such as Vanta, Drata or SafeBase) with these sections:
1. **Plain-language summary:** what PHI Drawbridge touches (fax images, lab results, caller identity) and why.
2. **HIPAA:** BAA available (link to the request form), the Privacy and Security Rule safeguards in place, and breach-notification commitments.
3. **Data handling:** encryption in transit and at rest (state actual versions and algorithms), retention windows for fax images, audio and transcripts, deletion on contract end, and whether data trains models.
4. **Access and audit:** least privilege, MFA, a per-action audit log visible to the practice.
5. **AI safety:** confidence thresholds, human review queue, identity-verification steps on calls and escalation rules.
6. **Infrastructure and subprocessors:** hosting provider, telephony and fax vendors, and LLM vendors under BAA, as a listed table.
7. **Certifications and roadmap:** list only what is achieved. Use "SOC 2 Type II: audit in progress, expected [date]" only if that is true. Never show a badge before the report exists.
8. **Vulnerability reporting:** a security contact plus a responsible-disclosure statement.
9. **Documents on request:** security questionnaire answers and policies, gated by a short request form.

Keep every security statement in one source-of-truth file so the homepage, FAQ and trust page never disagree (avoid Freed's drift).

### 6.5 ROI calculator
- **Inputs (practice-entered, with editable placeholders rather than claims):**
  - Faxes and lab results per day
  - Minutes to manually index and file each
  - Calls per day
  - Percentage unanswered or after-hours
  - Minutes per scheduling call
  - Loaded staff cost per hour
  - Average visit revenue
  - Percentage of missed calls that would have booked
- **Outputs:**
  - Staff hours per week returned (fax plus phone)
  - Dollar value of those hours
  - Appointments recovered per month, with revenue range
  - FTE-equivalent
- **Rules:**
  - Show the formula under each output.
  - Let users change the automation rate assumption, defaulting to a conservative value.
  - Label any default drawn from Drawbridge's synthetic tests as "synthetic test default".
  - Add the disclaimer "estimates only; not a guarantee".
  - No email gate to see results. Optionally offer "email me this estimate".
- **Placement:** a homepage section plus its own page. CTA under the results: "Book a walkthrough to validate these numbers".

### 6.6 Pricing presentation
Freed shows that small practices respond to transparency. If pricing is not final, publish a **"How pricing works"** block instead: per-provider or per-location, what's included (setup, shadow week, support), no long-term contract if true, and "Book a walkthrough for a quote". Publish real numbers once they are set.

### 6.7 Demo form: field count
**5 required fields plus 1 optional field.**
1. Full name
2. Work email
3. Practice name
4. EHR/PM in use (dropdown with Veradigm variants plus Other)
5. Number of providers (1–3, 4–10, 11–25, 26+)
6. Optional: phone number and "What's your biggest bottleneck?" (Fax/lab intake, Phone volume, Both)

Other form rules:
- Embed a calendar scheduler after submit so the visitor books immediately.
- Add a 3-bullet "what to expect" panel beside the form: a 20-minute call, a live fax and call demo on synthetic data, and a discussion of a shadow-mode pilot.
- Include a privacy line that says not to include patient information in the form.
- Do not collect PHI.

### 6.8 Claim-hygiene checklist (pre-launch)
- Every number has a source link, or a "synthetic test" label with N, date and method.
- No logos, testimonials, awards or "trusted by" language until real customers grant written permission.
- Veradigm name and logo usage follows Veradigm's approved wording. Do not use "partner" or "certified" until confirmed.
- No badge (HIPAA "certified", SOC 2) that cannot be backed. HIPAA has no certification, so say "designed for HIPAA compliance; BAA available".
- Server-render all numbers so nothing ever shows "0".
- One CTA label set, used site-wide.
