# Why the launch brand reads as amateur

Reviewed Oct 17, 2026: `brand/BRAND_KIT.md`, `brand/logo/*`, `src/assets/css/site.css`, the built pages and every file in `docs/screenshots/`. The site is honest and careful in its copy. The problems are visual, and most come from stacking the default moves of AI-generated sites on top of each other.

## The mark

- **It is clip art.** A castle tower with battlements, an arched door, a tilted plank, a chain, a dotted arc, a wave and an amber dot: seven ideas in a 64 px square. Good marks carry one idea. At 32 px the chain and dotted arc turn to noise, and at 16 px it's a purple blob with a yellow speck.
- **It is illustrated, not drawn.** The tower is a rectangle with notches, the deck is a rotated rounded rectangle and the wave is a repeated `q` curve. Nothing shares a stroke width, a radius or a grid, so it feels assembled rather than designed.
- **It doesn't relate to MOATiT.** MOATiT's own logo is bold geometric capitals, a shield and broken concentric arcs. The Drawbridge mark borrows none of that, so the endorsement "A MOATiT Service" has to do all the work of saying these are sister brands.

## The type

- **Three families plus italics.** Newsreader (serif display), Public Sans (body) and IBM Plex Mono (labels). Newsreader with one italic phrase per headline ("*Nothing guessed.*", "*Done carefully.*", "*clearly labeled.*", "*a week of proof.*") is the single most recognizable AI-landing-page tic of 2025 and 2026. It appears in five headlines on the home page alone.
- **Monospace eyebrows everywhere.** "01 SEE IT WORK", "03 THE DRAWBRIDGE RULES" and "RULE 01" (each set in mono capitals with a rule and a dash). Numbered mono labels are a developer-tool trope. For a clinic owner they read as decoration, not information.
- **None of it is MOATiT.** MOATiT uses Poppins in its documents and Barlow on moatit.com. The Drawbridge site uses neither, so nothing on the page carries over from the parent brand.

## Color

- **Indigo-purple, not navy.** The launch palette uses MOATiT's indigo correctly, then deepens it to "Night" `#1C1C45`, a violet-black. Behind a sky-blue italic headline and amber pill buttons, it lands on the purple-and-cyan look of generic AI products rather than MOATiT's navy, blue and gold.
- **Every accent is in use at once.** Sky for italics and eyebrows, amber for buttons, rule numbers and pins, green for checkmarks, red for warnings, a beige "SYNTHETIC SAMPLE" chip, plus a cream page (`#F7F5F0`) and a darker beige band. With that many colors, none of them signals anything.
- **A cream background.** Paper `#F7F5F0` plus a beige section band is the 2025 "warm editorial AI" default. MOATiT's own site and documents are on white.

## Layout and spacing

- **The same skeleton, repeated.** Eyebrow, two-line headline with an italic tail, gray lede, then a grid of rounded white cards. That repeats in roughly nine sections down a 13,900 px home page. Nothing changes pace, so nothing stands out.
- **Card soup.** Rounded 16 px cards with hairline borders hold features, stats, steps, values and FAQs alike, and the pastel icon tiles inside them (bell, sun, moon, gauge) are stock line icons.
- **A busy hero.** There's a top utility bar, then a nav, then a mono pill eyebrow, a three-line headline, a five-line paragraph, two pill buttons, a phone line, an AI-generated photo of a bridge and a hospital, a grid overlay, a sample card with four checks and a footnote, and finally a four-column trust strip. That's about twelve elements competing in the first screen.
- **AI-generated photography.** The bridge-at-dusk hero and the "front desk" image are visibly generated: plastic water, a melted railing and a building that isn't a building. Clinic owners notice. It undercuts the "nothing guessed" promise more than anything else on the page.
- **Pill buttons with a drop shadow** in amber, plus a ghost pill beside it. That's the default SaaS hero.

## Copy patterns (layout-level only; the substance is good)

- Every headline is built as "Statement. *Italic twist.*"
- Paragraphs run 4 to 6 lines where 2 would do. The product's best lines ("Two identifiers, one patient." "Read it back. Wait for a yes.") are buried in a dark band halfway down the page.

## What carries forward

- The idea: one controlled crossing; nothing reaches a chart or a caller unless it passes the checks.
- The honesty system: labeled synthetic numbers with N, dates and method.
- The rules list. It's the strongest content on the site and deserves to be the design, not a section.
