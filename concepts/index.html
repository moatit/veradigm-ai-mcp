Why Ledger

Ali's complaint was that the launch brand looks immature and AI-generated. The cure isn't a nicer gradient. It's specificity. Ledger's hero is the product doing its job: five synthetic faxes, two filed and three handed to a person with the reason. No stock moves can fake that, and it's exactly what a practice manager wants to see before trusting software with charts.

It's also the most MOATiT of the three in behavior, not just color: precise, accountable and plain-spoken. The ringed D borrows MOATiT's arcs, so the family tie shows without the endorsement.

If Ali prefers a calmer, more familiar look, Gatehouse is the safe choice and its D monogram is the strongest mark at 16 px. Night Watch is the most striking, but it's dark-first for an audience that mostly reads under office lighting, so I'd keep it for campaign pages rather than the main site.

Design bans held across all three

  • No cream or off-white page background
  • No italic accent words in headlines; no gradient text
  • No numbered section labels, no monospace eyebrows
  • No pill buttons, no emoji or stock icon tiles
  • No rows of identical feature cards; no glass; no purple-to-blue gradients
  • No AI photography; no third-party logos; no vendor names
  • No centered two-button hero; no section skeleton twice in a row
  • Copy without em dashes or the banned marketing words
  • Synthetic samples labeled; numbers carry their method
